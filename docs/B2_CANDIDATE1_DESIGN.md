## Causal input, output and task record

The generated lane-one tap-note renderer exposes only current visibility and normalized current screen position `p∈[0,1]`. It may compute that frame from its own note schedule; the fixed encoder receives only the rendered observation. It maps each sorted KC ID by rank modulo 16 to a Gaussian position center, sampled every 1 ms, with fixed 25-ms sensory latency and peak drive 2.0 normalized threshold units. A visible note consumed by judgement or expired stops new position drive and cancels its queued artificial input. The renderer and encoder log both the presented position and emitted KC drive. No target key, exact future timestamp, countdown, desired press time, judgement or reward enters this path. Use isolated notes: 800-ms visible travel from screen entry to nominal hit position, at least 1,200 ms between entries, no overlapping cue. This is a **task renderer assumption** and must be identical in training and frozen evaluation; future validation across speeds is separate.

The sole output is DNp42 10713. Two spikes in the previous 20 ms make lane-one DOWN; zero spikes after the 10-ms minimum hold releases UP, with 30-ms maximum hold and 200-ms cooldown. The sign of the keyboard mapping is a fixed engineering choice. There is no learned decoder. For each note, its first DOWN from visibility onset until the next isolated cue is the primary action, even if it is a null press or occurs after expiry. A DOWN before visibility is a background action; later DOWNs never replace the first. No DOWN is a failure. Primary success means first DOWN within **±73,000 µs** of nominal hit time; the denominator includes every note. Raw key/game events, signed error, judgement, null presses, extra DOWNs and no-DOWN are logged separately. A3 must implement and independently reconstruct this contract before any learning run.

## Local rule and reinforcement timing

At each selected KC terminal, a causal KC trace `x_i` decays with 1-s constant; a γ4 PAM trace `d` decays with 1.2-s constant. Both are bounded in [0,1]. PAM activity is the nonnegative fraction of 25 actual PAM08 spikes in a tick. At a KC spike, the *pre-event* PAM trace potentiates only selected contact contribution; at a PAM spike, the *pre-event* KC trace depresses it. A simultaneous KC/PAM event uses the specified depression branch, consistent with the reported γ4 coincidence direction. Each signed update magnitude is 0.001 of that contact's initial coupling times the corresponding trace/event factor, clipped to 0.5–1.5× initial positive magnitude. No postsynaptic MBON spike is required. The event scheduler processes arrivals and a simultaneous spike batch once, then computes updates from the traces immediately before that batch and commits traces/weights once. Zero PAM or no eligible trace produces no update. This is a causal engineering approximation of the experimental polarity; the 1-s traces are not fitted to the game's ±73-ms window.

The feedback pipeline records game judgement and **first-action** disposition separately. Utility is 1 for a successful first DOWN and 0 for early, late or missing action. A prior expected utility starts at 1 and is updated with fixed EMA α=0.1 *after* computing `RPE = utility − expected_before`. Artificial PAM request amplitude is `clip(−RPE,0,1)`. This is an explicitly task-designed deficit signal, not a measured dopamine prediction error. No negative dopamine is allowed; as a sustained failure lowers expectation, the signal can weaken and the candidate may fail.

Feedback timing supplies the rule's two directions without changing its sign. A judged **early** first press consumes the note; after the fixed sensory latency flushes queued cue drive, a nonnegative PAM pulse meets prior KC traces and tends to depress the active terminals. A **late** first press or **no DOWN** stores the past outcome category and amplitude; at the *next actually visible note onset*, the PAM pulse starts while that new cue's KC drive waits for its fixed sensory latency, so subsequent KC spikes can potentiate. This is cross-trial credit for a repeated lane-one cue class. A too-early null press remains a counted failure but receives no pulse because continuing cue input would mix polarities; this is a known blind spot. A successful first DOWN receives no task pulse. A pending late prime with no next visible cue is discarded and logged. PAM drive is an artificial 20-ms, two-threshold-unit current pulse scaled by the request; updates use actual PAM spikes, never the requested amplitude directly.

With the declared double-inhibitory downstream hypothesis, stronger KC→MBON05 output should promote DNp42 activity: depression should delay an early action, potentiation should advance a late or missing one. This directional argument is **INFERRED** and is the candidate's most important falsifiable prediction. Delayed PAM spikes might miss the intended order, and the bypass/background may dominate; B3 measures both. The next-cue prime makes this a supervised, artificial teaching interface with across-trial memory. A static LIF timing check gives ~13.9 ms to threshold from rest at two threshold units (τ=20 ms); the fixed 25-ms sensory delay can therefore place an initial PAM spike before the new cue's first directly driven KC spike. This is an equation check, not a neural-run result. Background, recurrent drive, pulse amplitude and later spikes may change the order, which B3 must measure. It does not establish that a living fly uses this task feedback.
